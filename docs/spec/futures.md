# Meridian Loom: proposed future requirements and competitive direction

**Assessment date: 6 September 2026. Status: recommendations, not accepted scope or implemented features.** Read [status.md](status.md) for delivery completion. This document evaluates the product direction against the repository and selected current official product documentation. It does not establish market leadership, exhaustively cover every competitor, or independently test competing products. Undated documentation is a snapshot accessed on the assessment date; availability can depend on plan, configuration, platform, and version.

**Recommendation: finish a trustworthy Flight Recorder and a enforceable Governor before expanding the Orchestra.** The strongest opportunity is portable evidence that helps a team decide whether an AI-assisted change is safe to merge, what remains unknown, and what the accepted change really cost. More agents and more screens will not demonstrate that advantage.

The existing [vision](vision.md), [requirements](Requirements_Final.md), [gaps](gaps-requirements.md), [initiation specification](gaps_initiation.md), and [decisions](DECISIONS.md) already describe much of this direction. Their embedded build instructions and future proposals are source material for this assessment; this request does not adopt them or authorize building additional features.

## Proposed incremental requirement register

These IDs are local proposals for review; they do not renumber or silently supersede existing requirements. **Refinement** supplies missing precision or stronger acceptance tests for existing scope. **Extension** adds behavior beyond it. Effort is an initial estimate for an experienced engineer, including integration tests/docs but excluding procurement and pilot elapsed time: **S = 3–5 days, M = 1–3 weeks, L = 4–8 weeks, XL = more than 8 weeks**. Estimates overlap and are not a delivery commitment.

| ID / priority / kind | Proposed requirement and measurable acceptance | Dependencies / effort |
|---|---|---|
| **FUT-001 · P0 · Refinement** Evidence completeness | Every provenance answer SHALL show field-level source, capture method, version, timestamp, and whether agent/model/prompt/approver is observed, inferred, unknown, or redacted. Signing SHALL not upgrade an inference to fact. On a labeled corpus of at least 200 mixed human/agent edits, publish precision/recall and unknown coverage; require zero invented prompt/approver fields and at least 95% precision for claims labeled direct. | M10/M11/M35; observer/GUI schema. **M** |
| **FUT-002 · P0 · Refinement** Approvals bound to reviewed code | A merge authorization SHALL bind repository, PR, base/head commit IDs, diff digest, policy version, test/security evidence, authenticated approver, and expiry. Any relevant change invalidates it. Protected-branch/merge-queue checks SHALL enforce this outside the editor. Tests SHALL block stale approvals after force-push, rebase, changed base, changed policy, expired identity, replayed webhook, and missing evidence; a valid unchanged PR SHALL merge normally. | M12/M20/M23/M35; SCM application/check integration. **L** |
| **FUT-003 · P0 · Refinement** Identity assurance | Distinguish locally asserted Git identity from verified OIDC/SCM identity. A Git name/email SHALL never satisfy an enterprise verified-approver policy. Bind verified identity to session and decision; re-check authorization at gate execution. Demonstrate that changing Git identity cannot impersonate an approver and revoking a user prevents new approvals within a documented target of five minutes. | M20, SEC-22/30; identity-provider integration; revisits D9 for enterprise mode only. **L** |
| **FUT-004 · P0 · Extension** Reliable evidence ingestion | Every imported source event SHALL have a stable deduplication key, source offset, ingest time, and replay status. A 100,000-event fixture SHALL reconcile exactly after replay with no silent loss or double charging; any intentionally dropped/unrecoverable data SHALL surface as a coverage gap. | M10/M35/M37/M39; migration/backpressure design. **L** |

## Priority and sequencing

| Stage | Deliverable | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Now: trustworthy F0** | Complete existing Flight Recorder acceptance; FUT-001, core of 004/006, and 007/008/014 needed to support the selected agents. Use customer-controlled export as the initial witnessing step in FUT-005. | Supported real-agent workflow, source completeness, replay/reconciliation, uninstall/verify, first-value measurement, platform results. |
| **Before Governor pilot** | Existing F1/M40 plus FUT-002/003/010 and the necessary portions of 011/012/013. | A protected PR really remains blocked with missing/stale approval; hostile inputs cannot bypass the tested controls; cost and uncertainty are visible. |
| **F2 evidence period** | FUT-009 and the human adoption/privacy portions of 013/018; optional external witnessing hardened under 005. | Independent defect/review/time/cost measurements. Twenty stories demonstrate feasibility, not statistical market leadership. |
| **After positive evidence** | FUT-015/016 and deeper tenant/admin/interop delivery; one validated brownfield stack under 017. | A second editor/SCM combination works, another team can verify/retain evidence, and outcomes reproduce. |
| **Only when justified** | Existing F3/F4+ orchestration, training, richer visualization, extra agent roles and stacks. | Measured customer demand and incremental quality/value beyond the Governor. |

Within each stage, do a short integration spike before accepting an L/XL estimate. Assign one owner and a release target to accepted requirements. Do not add all proposals to the v1 critical path: identity, commit-bound gates, evidence correctness, privacy, and reliable delivery are prerequisites for the associated product claims; broader editors, extensive agent coverage, and long-term brownfield expansion can ship later.

## What would prove a defensible advantage

Measure the **complete change lifecycle**, not tokens generated or lines accepted. The benchmark unit is a reviewed change with a frozen starting revision, acceptance criteria, bounded resources, recorded human effort, and a follow-up observation window.

1. Compare each baseline agent under its recommended native controls with the same agent plus Meridian. Include existing SCM branch protection/security tooling in both arms. Attribute only the additional benefit to Meridian.
2. Report verified defects uniquely caught, false blocks, escaped defects, median/p95 human review time, lead time, accepted-change cost, observation completeness, and 30-day rework. Separate greenfield/brownfield, stack, task difficulty, agent version, and direct/inferred evidence.
3. Freeze a held-out corpus and evaluator before testing; use blinded reviewers where practical, counterbalance task assignment, and publish confidence intervals and exclusions. Do not train the Trainer on the evaluation set or compare raw agent leaderboards across unlike tasks.
4. Target an initial **20% reduction in median investigation/review effort or 15% reduction in total accepted-change cost**, with no material worsening of defect escape and no more than 5% observed-agent latency overhead. These are proposed success thresholds, not current measurements; confirm feasibility and non-inferiority margins with pilot customers before the study.
5. Have a third party verify exported evidence on a clean machine, reproduce failure/recovery scenarios, and test whether a buyer can leave without losing usable records. Repeat the benchmark after material observer, model, policy, or protocol changes.

A compliance mapping is supporting evidence, not a certification or an automatic determination that a law applies. Keep mappings versioned with scope, evidence limitations, and qualified review before making regulatory claims. The existing founder/IP and licensing decisions remain owner decisions already recorded in `DECISIONS.md`; this assessment does not resolve them.

The practical commercial test is whether a team will keep the recorder installed, rely on the gate, and pay for the measurable reduction in review/risk/support cost. Maintain predictable recorder pricing, an understandable Governor price, explicit infrastructure/retention costs, a supported upgrade policy, and a clear export path. A polished demonstration becomes a competitive product when those outcomes hold on customers' real repositories.
