# Meridian Loom: proposed future requirements and competitive direction

**Assessment date: 9 September 2026. Status: recommendations, not accepted scope or implemented features.** Read [status.md](status.md) for delivery completion. This document evaluates the product direction against the repository at commit `4b79bef` and against official product documentation accessed on 6–9 September 2026. It does not establish market leadership, exhaustively cover every competitor, or independently test competing products. Undated documentation is a snapshot from that access window; availability can depend on plan, configuration, platform and version.

**Recommendation, revised. The Governor is now built but largely invisible, and its analytics are silently partial. Finish those two things before building anything else.** The 7 September recommendation — "finish validating the Flight Recorder and deliver an enforceable Governor" — has been substantially met on the backend: policy gates, merge approval bound to a head-commit digest, halt, external-PR ingest, roles with separation of duties and N-of-M, steer, the full trust-analytics set and cross-vendor spend all exist and are tested. Three things now stand between that work and a product a team would pay for, and none of them is a new feature:

1. **Every analytic silently truncates at 1,000 ledger rows** (status.md finding G-01). Rejection rate, reason distribution, trust score, agent comparison, the J-curve and the DORA export all request exactly 1,000 rows with no pagination and no truncation flag. On any real repository these numbers are wrong and nothing says so.
2. **Seven of the ten trust RPCs and all five spend RPCs have no caller in the interface** (finding G-02). The trust score, the J-curve, tokenmaxxing and cross-vendor spend are the differentiating instruments in the thesis, and today a user cannot see any of them.
3. **No measurement requiring a real team has been performed** — first value, twenty stories, competitor parity, cross-platform, assistive technology. F2 is correctly recorded as pending-human-evidence under D35, but that means the central product claim is still unevidenced.

The strongest remaining opportunity is unchanged and is now sharper than in September's first draft: **portable, independently verifiable evidence that survives the vendor, bound to the merge decision it justified.** Every adjacent product reviewed below either records administrative events rather than produced code, or estimates rather than reconciles, or retains for months rather than years, or asserts immutability without a verification mechanism. That is the gap. More agents and more screens will not widen it.

The existing [vision](vision.md), [requirements](Requirements_Final.md), [gaps](gaps-requirements.md), [initiation specification](gaps_initiation.md), and [decisions](DECISIONS.md) already describe much of this direction. Their embedded build instructions and future proposals are source material for this assessment; this document does not adopt them or authorise building additional features.

### Standards

ACP remains a sensible interoperability choice. Its v1 documentation describes single-integer protocol versioning, **optional** agent identity, and remote transport still in progress; the ACP Registry format documents distribution metadata with **no publisher signing** and unpinned `npx` entries. MCP's 2026-07-28 revision made the protocol stateless; its authorization model mandates audience binding but is optional overall and absent on stdio, and the MCP roadmap names **agent identity and delegation as an unsolved priority area**, while the official registry states that namespace verification is explicitly not a trust signal. OpenTelemetry's GenAI semantic conventions remain at "Development" status with documented rename risk. AGENTS.md is a convention with no schema, no version and no enforcement. [ACP init](https://agentclientprotocol.com/protocol/initialization), [ACP registry format](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/registry/blob/main/FORMAT.md), [MCP authorization](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/authorization), [MCP roadmap](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/development/roadmap), [MCP registry](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/about)

Implement a **named supported protocol version and capability set** and record it per ledger entry. "ACP won" is not a technical acceptance criterion, and neither protocol currently solves agent identity — which means Meridian must (FUT-028) rather than assume the standard will.

**Refresh the star-ranking table in `gaps-requirements.md` before using it externally.** Its "no one else" and "structurally cannot" claims are contradicted by this review: Cursor ships per-line AI blame, Datadog ships cross-vendor agent spend in preview, GitHub manages third-party agents, and DX ships an attribution daemon. Market size, adoption, rejection-rate and productivity numbers quoted in earlier project documents were not independently validated here and should not become marketing claims without their original studies, dates, definitions and populations.

## New proposed requirements

These continue the local numbering at `FUT-019`. **Refinement** supplies missing precision or a stronger acceptance test for existing scope; **Extension** adds behaviour beyond it. Effort is an initial estimate for an experienced engineer including integration tests and documentation, excluding procurement and pilot elapsed time: **S = 3–5 days, M = 1–3 weeks, L = 4–8 weeks, XL = more than 8 weeks**. Estimates overlap and are not a delivery commitment.

| ID / priority / kind | Proposed requirement and measurable acceptance | Dependencies / effort |
|---|---|---|
| **FUT-019 · P0 · Refinement** Unattributed is a first-class state | Attribution SHALL report three states — `agent`, `human`, and `unattributed` — and SHALL NEVER derive one from the others by subtraction. Every attribution result SHALL carry the proportion in each state and the reason each unattributed span could not be resolved (no signal, formatter rewrite, squashed history, pre-installation, unsupported vendor). Every trust metric SHALL be reported alongside its attribution coverage, and a metric computed over less than a configurable coverage floor SHALL read `insufficient_coverage`, not a number. On a labelled corpus of at least 200 mixed spans, publish the three-way confusion matrix. **Cursor's documented residual formula makes any unresolved line count as human; Meridian must not repeat it.** | `attribution/*`, `metrics/*`; FUT-001. **M** |
| **FUT-020 · P0 · Extension** Full-history analytics with declared coverage | No analytic SHALL silently truncate. `ledger.query` SHALL support cursor pagination beyond its current 1,000-row clamp, and every analytic result SHALL carry `rowsConsidered`, `rowsAvailable`, `truncated` and the sequence range covered. A CI test SHALL build a 50,000-entry ledger, compute every trust and spend metric, and assert each result equals an independent full-scan recomputation. Any surface displaying a truncated figure SHALL say so in text, as the Cross-Vendor Spend panel already does. **This is status.md finding G-01 and the single highest-value correctness fix in the codebase.** | `ledger/core.py`, all six `metrics/*` consumers; narrowest form of FUT-004. **M** |

## What would prove a defensible advantage

Measure the **complete change lifecycle**, not tokens generated or lines accepted. The benchmark unit is a reviewed change with a frozen starting revision, acceptance criteria, bounded resources, recorded human effort and a follow-up observation window.

1. Compare each baseline agent under its own recommended native controls against the same agent plus Meridian. Include existing SCM branch protection and security tooling in both arms. Attribute only the additional benefit to Meridian.
2. Report verified defects uniquely caught, false blocks, escaped defects, median and p95 human review time, lead time, accepted-change cost, observation completeness and 30-day rework. Separate greenfield from brownfield, stack, task difficulty, agent version, and direct from inferred evidence.
3. Freeze a held-out corpus and evaluator before testing; use blinded reviewers where practical, counterbalance task assignment, and publish confidence intervals and exclusions. Do not train on the evaluation set and do not compare raw agent leaderboards across unlike tasks.
4. Target an initial **20% reduction in median investigation and review effort, or 15% reduction in total accepted-change cost**, with no material worsening of defect escape and no more than 5% observed-agent latency overhead. These are proposed thresholds, not measurements; confirm feasibility and non-inferiority margins with pilot customers before the study.
5. Have a third party verify exported evidence on a clean machine, reproduce failure and recovery scenarios, and test whether a buyer can leave without losing usable records. Repeat after any material observer, model, policy or protocol change.

A compliance mapping is supporting evidence, not a certification and not a determination that a law applies. Keep mappings versioned with scope, evidence limitations and qualified review before making regulatory claims. The founder, IP and licensing decisions remain owner decisions recorded in `DECISIONS.md`; this assessment does not resolve them, and `D22` remains open.

The practical commercial test is whether a team will keep the recorder installed, rely on the gate, and pay for a measurable reduction in review, risk and support cost. Maintain predictable recorder pricing, an understandable Governor price, explicit infrastructure and retention costs, a supported upgrade policy and a clear export path. A polished demonstration becomes a competitive product when those outcomes hold on customers' real repositories — and the evidence in this review says the way there is narrower and deeper than the original roadmap assumed.
